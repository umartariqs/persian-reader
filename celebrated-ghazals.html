Hafez

الا یا ایها الساقی

Ganjoor source

Come, cupbearer: turn the cup and pass it round—for love seemed easy at first, but then its difficulties arose.

In hope that at last the breeze will loosen a musk-pod from those tresses—what blood has fallen into hearts from the curling of those musky locks!

What security can I have in the beloved’s stopping-place, when at every moment the bell cries, ‘Fasten the camel-litters’!

If the Magian elder commands it, stain the prayer-rug with wine; for the traveller on the road is not unaware of the customs of its stages.

A dark night, fear of the waves, and so terrifying a whirlpool—how can those lightly burdened on the shores understand our condition?

Every undertaking to which self-will led me finally brought disgrace. How could the secret concealed in assemblies remain hidden?

If you desire his presence, Hafez, do not be absent from him; when you meet the one you desire, abandon the world and let it go.

Hafez

اگر آن ترک شیرازی

Ganjoor source

If that Shirazi Turk would take our heart in hand, I would give Samarkand and Bukhara for the Hindu mole upon her cheek.

Cupbearer, give the remaining wine—for in Paradise you will not find the banks of the Ruknabad or the rose-garden of Musalla.

!

Alas! Those playful, sweet-working gypsies have so carried patience from my heart that Turks might carry off a banquet’s plunder.

Our imperfect love has no need of the beloved’s beauty: a lovely face has no need of water, colour, mole, or painted line.

From the beauty of Joseph, which increased day by day, I knew that love would draw Zuleikha out from behind chastity’s curtain.

If you curse me or pray for me, I shall answer you with blessing: bitter words suit those ruby, sugar-scattering lips.

Listen to counsel, my soul, for fortunate young people cherish the advice of a wise elder more dearly than life itself.

Speak of musicians and wine, and seek the secret of time less—for no one has solved, or ever will solve, this mystery through wisdom.

You have spoken a ghazal and pierced pearls—come, sing it sweetly, Hafez, so heaven may scatter the necklace of the Pleiades upon your verse.

Hafez

دوش دیدم که ملائک در میخانه زدند

Ganjoor source

Last night I saw the angels knock at the tavern door: they kneaded Adam’s clay and cast it into the wine-cup.

The inhabitants of heaven’s sanctuary of concealment and chastity drank intoxicating wine with me, a roadside wanderer.

Heaven could not bear the burden of the Trust; they drew the lot for the task in the name of mad me.

Excuse all the warfare of the seventy-two sects: because they did not see the truth, they set out upon the road of fable.

Thanks be to God that peace arose between me and Him; the Sufis danced and raised the cup of thanksgiving.

Fire is not that from whose flame the candle smiles; fire is that which they set in the moth’s harvest.

No one like Hafez unveiled the face of thought, once they combed the curling locks of speech with the pen.

Hafez

سال‌ها دل طلب جام جم از ما می‌کرد

Ganjoor source

For years the heart sought Jamshid’s world-disclosing cup from us, begging from strangers for what it possessed itself.

It sought from those lost along the seashore a pearl that lies beyond the shell of time and space.

 

Last night I took my difficulty before the Magian elder, who solved riddles with the confirmation of his insight.

I saw him cheerful and laughing, a wine-cup in hand, contemplating a hundred sights within that mirror.

I asked, ‘When did the Sage give you this world-seeing cup?’ He said, ‘On the day He fashioned this azure dome.’

A man bereft of heart had God with him in every state; he did not see Him, and called upon God from afar.

All the sorcery the Samaritan performed here he performed before Moses’ staff and shining white hand.

He spoke of that friend through whom the gallows gained distinction: his crime was that he made the mysteries public.

If the grace of the Holy Spirit should again lend aid, others too will do what the Messiah did.

I asked him, ‘What is the chain of the idols’ tresses for?’ He said, ‘Hafez was voicing a complaint from his love-distraught heart.’

Hafez

در ازل پرتو حسنت ز تجلی دم زد

Ganjoor source

In pre-eternity the ray of your beauty breathed forth in self-disclosure; love appeared and set the whole world aflame.

Your face revealed a splendour; the angel saw it and possessed no love, became pure fire from jealousy, and struck at Adam.

Reason wished to kindle a lamp from that flame; the lightning of jealousy flashed and threw the world into confusion.

The pretender wished to enter the viewing-place of the mystery; the hand of the unseen came and struck the stranger’s breast.

All others cast the lot of their portion upon pleasure; it was our grief-stricken heart that cast its lot upon grief.

The celestial soul desired the well of your chin; it put its hand into the ring of those curl upon curl tresses.

Hafez wrote the book of joy of your love on the day the pen crossed out all the means of the cheerful heart.

Hafez

یوسف گمگشته بازآید به کنعان غم مخور

Ganjoor source

The lost Joseph will return to Canaan—do not grieve; the cottage of sorrows will one day become a rose-garden—do not grieve.

O grief-stricken heart, your condition will improve; do not lose heart. This bewildered head will again come to order—do not grieve.

If the springtime of life remains, you will again draw the rose’s parasol over your head upon the garden throne, O sweet-singing bird—do not grieve.

If the turning heavens did not go according to our wish for two days, the condition of time never remains the same—do not grieve.

Take care not to despair, since you do not know the mystery of the unseen; there may be hidden games behind the curtain—do not grieve.

O heart, if annihilation’s flood uproots the foundation of existence, since Noah is your helmsman, do not fear the storm.

If you would set foot in the desert from longing for the Kaaba, though the thorns of the desert rebuke you, do not grieve.

Although the stopping-place is very dangerous and the destination very distant, there is no road that has no end—do not grieve.

God, who transforms all states, knows entirely our condition in separation from the beloved and the rival’s harassment—do not grieve.

Hafez, in poverty’s corner and the solitude of dark nights, so long as prayer and study of the Quran are your litany, do not grieve.

Saadi

به جهان خرم از آنم که جهان خرم از اوست

Ganjoor source

I rejoice in the world because the world rejoices through Him; I love the whole universe because the whole universe is from Him.

Treasure, O friend, the morning’s Jesus-like breath, so that perhaps you may revive a dead heart—for this breath comes from Him.

Neither the heavens securely possess, nor the angels attain, what lies from Him within the innermost secret of humankind.

I swallow poison sweetly when the beloved is cupbearer; I willingly bear pain when the cure also comes from Him.

If my bleeding wound does not improve, it is better so: blessed is the wound whose balm comes to me from Him at every moment.

What difference do grief and joy make to the knower? Cupbearer, give wine—joyous is even this grief, since it comes from Him.

Kingship and beggary are alike to us, for at this door everyone’s back is bent in worship of Him.

Saadi, if annihilation’s flood uproots the house of life, keep a strong heart: the foundation of permanence is made firm by Him.

Saadi

من ندانستم از اول که تو بی مهر و وفایی

Ganjoor source

I did not know at first that you were without love or fidelity; it is better not to make a covenant than to make one and fail to keep it.

My friends blame me for giving you my heart; they should first ask you why you are so beautiful.

You who told me not to pursue the beauties of the age—where are we in this sea of reflection, and where are you?

It is not merely mole, chin, and dishevelled tresses that stole discerning hearts: it is a divine mystery.

Lift the veil, for a stranger cannot truly see this face; you are great and cannot appear within a little mirror.

Because of the rivals I cannot knock at your door; all I can do is come to your quarter as a beggar.

Love, dervishhood, public notoriety, and blame are all easy; it is the burden of separation that I cannot endure.

It is a day for the countryside, music, the stream-bank, and gazing; no heart remains in the whole city for you to steal again.

I had said that when you came I would tell you my heart’s grief; what can I say, when the grief leaves my heart as soon as you arrive?

The candle must be carried from this house and extinguished, lest it tell the neighbour that you are in our house.

Saadi is not one ever to escape your lasso, for he knows that captivity to you is sweeter than freedom.

People say, ‘Go, give your heart to another desire.’ I will not—especially in the days of Atabak—practice double allegiance.

Saadi

دیده از دیدار خوبان برگرفتن مشکل است

Ganjoor source

It is difficult to avert one’s eyes from seeing beautiful people; whoever gives us this advice gives it fruitlessly.

Though a beautiful beloved may put a thousand fears of him into your heart, seeing his face at dawn is an auspicious morning.

The helpless lovers’ hearts are imprisoned in the well of his chin, like angels confined in Babylon’s well.

Formerly I used to claim abstinence; now I say that every claim I made was false.

Though poison is deadly in the judgement of the wise, when you take it from the beloved’s hand it is an immediate cure.

I cannot bring myself to set foot outside the beloved’s quarter; friends, excuse me—my foot is stuck in the mud.

Let all the wise call me mad: one cannot renounce life until you declare that reasonable.

The person who says looking upon beautiful forms is wrong sees only the form itself and remains heedless of its meaning.

!

Camel-driver, go slowly, for my soul’s repose is in the litter: the beasts carry burdens on their backs, but we carry ours upon the heart.

Though a hundred stages of separation fall between us and the beloved, his dwelling remains within the midst of the sweet soul.

!

Saadi, making friends with anyone is easy; but once the bond is formed, breaking the habit is difficult.

Rumi

مرده بدم زنده شدم گریه بدم خنده شدم

Ganjoor source

I was dead and became alive; I was weeping and became laughter. Love’s fortune arrived, and I became enduring good fortune.

My eye is satisfied, my soul is bold; I have a lion’s courage, and I have become a shining Venus.

He said, ‘You are not mad; you are not fit for this house.’ I went and became mad, a binder of chains.

He said, ‘You are not utterly drunk; go, for you are not of this company.’ I went, became drunk, and was filled with joy.

He said, ‘You have not been slain; you are not steeped in joy.’ Before his life-giving face I became slain and cast down.

He said, ‘You are a little clever fellow, drunk on imagination and doubt.’ I became a fool, became bewildered, and was severed from everything.

He said, ‘You have become a candle, the qibla of this gathering.’ I am no gathering and no candle; I became scattered smoke.

He said, ‘You are an elder and chief, a leader and guide.’ I am no elder and no leader; I became the servant of your command.

He said, ‘You have wings and feathers; I shall not give you my pinions.’ In longing for his wings I became wingless and plucked.

New Fortune told me, ‘Do not travel and tire yourself, for through grace and generosity I have come toward you.’

Ancient Love told me, ‘Do not move away from beside us.’ I said, ‘Yes, I will not’; I became settled and abiding.

You are the fountain of the sun and I the shade of the willow; when you struck upon my head, I became lowly and melting.

My heart found the soul’s radiance; my heart opened and split apart. My heart wove new satin, and I became the enemy of this ragged cloak.

At dawn the soul’s form boasted from exuberance: I had been a servant and donkey-driver; I became king and lord.

Your paper gives thanks for your measureless sweetness: it came into my embrace, and I became like it.

The sullen earth gives thanks, bowing to heaven and the sphere, for through His gaze and turning I became receptive to light.

The celestial sphere gives thanks for king, kingdom, and angel, for through His generosity and giving I became radiant and giving.

The knower of Truth gives thanks that we surpassed everyone; above the seven levels I became a shining star.

I was Venus and became the moon; I became a heaven folded two hundred times. I was Joseph, and henceforth became one who brings forth Josephs.

I am from you, O renowned moon; look within me and within yourself, for through the effect of your laughter I became a laughing rose-garden.

Move like a chess-piece: be silent, yet entirely tongue—for through the face of that king of the world I became fortunate and blessed.

Rumi

بنمای رخ که باغ و گلستانم آرزوست

Ganjoor source

Show your face, for I desire garden and rose-garden; open your lips, for I desire abundant sugar.

!

O sun of beauty, come out from the cloud for a moment; I desire that radiant, gleaming face.

I heard the falcon-drum sounding through longing for you; I returned, for I desire the Sultan’s forearm.

« »
« »

Coquettishly you said, ‘Do not trouble me any more—go.’ I desire that very saying of yours, ‘Do not trouble me any more.’

« »

I desire that dismissal—‘Go, the king is not at home’—and the doorkeeper’s coyness, play, and harshness.

Whatever beauty anyone possesses is only a shaving; I desire that mine of loveliness, that treasure-vein.

The sphere’s bread and water are faithless as a flood; I am a fish, a whale—I desire the ocean of Oman.

« »

Like Jacob I continually cry ‘Alas!’; I desire the beautiful sight of Joseph of Canaan.

By God, the city without you becomes my prison; I desire wandering, mountain, and desert.

My heart is weary of these feeble-natured companions; I desire God’s lion and Rostam son of Dastan.

My soul has wearied of Pharaoh and his tyranny; I desire the light of Moses son of Imran’s face.

I have wearied of these complaining, weeping people; I desire the uproar and cries of the drunken.

I am more eloquent than the nightingale, but from fear of common envy a seal is on my mouth—and I desire lamentation.

Yesterday an elder wandered round the city with a lamp, saying, ‘I am weary of demons and beasts; I desire a human being.’

« »
« »

They said, ‘He cannot be found; we have searched.’ He said, ‘I desire the one who cannot be found.’

Though I am penniless, I will not accept a little carnelian; I desire that rare yet freely given carnelian.

Hidden from eyes, while every eye is from Him: I desire that manifest artisan who remains hidden.

My business has passed beyond every desire and greed; beyond mine and place, I desire the source of all foundations.

My ear heard the tale of faith and became drunk; where is the eye’s share? I desire faith’s visible form.

A wine-cup in one hand and the beloved’s curl in the other—I desire such a dance in the middle of the field.

That rebab says, ‘I have died of waiting; I desire Uthman’s hand, embrace, and plectrum.’

I too am love’s rebab, and love is a rebab-player; I desire the Merciful One’s gracious strokes.

O subtle minstrel, count the remainder of this ghazal in this same manner, for I desire it just so.

Rumi

ای عاشقان ای عاشقان امروز ماییم و شما

Ganjoor source

O lovers, O lovers, today it is we and you, fallen into a drowning-pool—let us see who truly knows how to swim.

If the world fills with flood and every wave becomes a camel, what grief have water-birds? Let the bird of the air grieve.

Our faces glow with thanksgiving; we are accustomed to wave and sea, just as sea and storm give life to the fish.

O sheikh, give us a bathing-cloth; O water, plunge us under. O Moses son of Imran, come strike the sea with your staff.

This wind cooks a different passion in every head; the passion for that cupbearer is mine—all the rest belongs to you.

Yesterday on the road that cupbearer snatched the drunkards’ caps; today he pours wine so that he may strip off our robes.

O envy of moon and Jupiter, with us yet hidden like a fairy: gently you draw me away, but will you not say where to?

Wherever you go, you are with me, O both my eyes and their light; draw me toward drunkenness if you wish, or carry me toward annihilation.

Know the world as Mount Sinai and us as Moses-like seekers: at every moment self-disclosure arrives and splits the mountain apart.

One piece becomes green, one becomes fragrant iris, one becomes jewel, and one becomes ruby and amber.

O seeker of His vision, look upon this mountain-range of His. O mountain, what wine have you drunk? We became intoxicated merely by the sound.

O gardener, O gardener, why have you tangled with us? If we have taken your grapes, you have taken our purse.

Attar

ای دل اگر عاشقی در پی دلدار باش

Ganjoor source

O heart, if you are a lover, pursue the beloved; wait at the heart’s door day and night for the friend.

Your beloved is always present at the heart’s door; go, open the door within the heart—be present and awake.

Until the soul’s eye sees His face openly, turn your face to the wall in your search for His face.

The army of the clamorous ego has seized the heart’s territory; therefore, if you are a lover, be a vigilant lover.

No one knows when the beloved will reveal his beauty; but for your part, be ready for the task here and now.

On His road spend whatever you possess, even heart and soul; you live through the One, so renounce all else.

If your heart and soul desire the pearl of permanence, say nothing and accompany Attar into annihilation.

Fakhr al-Din Iraqi

نخستین باده کاندر جام کردند

Ganjoor source

The first wine they placed within the cup they borrowed from the cupbearer’s drunken eye.

When they found the people of revelry still possessed of themselves, they poured selflessness’s wine into the cup.

The beloved’s wine-coloured lip passed round a cup; they named its wine ‘the lovers’ wine.’

To hunt the hearts of an entire world, they made the beautiful ones’ curling locks into snares.

Wherever in the world there was an aching heart, they gathered them together and named the whole collection love.

The idols’ curling tresses found no rest, from the multitude of hearts they made restless.

When they cast beauty’s ball onto the field, with a single charge they brought both worlds under command.

As relish for the drunkards they prepared pistachios and almonds from lip and eye.

From that lip—a fate worthy of a hundred praises—they assigned abuse as the loveless ones’ portion.

They gave both good and bad a place in the assembly; with one cup they attended to elite and common alike.

With a sidelong glance they spoke a hundred words to the soul; through the eyebrows they sent two hundred messages to the heart.

They displayed their own beauty; with a single manifestation they brought both worlds under command.

To capture a heart, at every moment they made the ends of their tresses into snares.

Secretly they told a mystery to one confidant, and through that act made it known to a whole world.

Since they themselves disclosed their own secret, why did they give Iraqi the bad name?

Jami

یا من بدا جمالک فی کل ما بدا

Ganjoor source

O you whose beauty appears in all that appears: may a thousand holy souls be sacrificed for you.

Moment by moment I lament separation from you like the reed—stranger still, I am not apart from you for a single breath.

It is love alone that manifests itself in both worlds, now in a king’s robes and now in a beggar’s garb.

One sound reaches your ear in two forms: sometimes you call it ‘summons,’ and sometimes ‘echo.’

Rise, cupbearer, and graciously pour a draught for the grief-stricken lovers from that grief-dispelling cup.

When that special cup delivers me from selfhood, nothing but God remains in the eye of direct vision.

Jami, there is no road of guidance to God except love. We have spoken—and peace be upon whoever follows guidance.
